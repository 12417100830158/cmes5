Q-DAY

Quantum Surveillance Policy Simulator
// About this tool

Created by: Mai — Computational Social Science, University of Amsterdam, 2026

The dilemma: Quantum computers, expected within the next decade, will be able to break all current encryption standards. Intelligence agencies are already collecting encrypted civilian data today — messages, medical records, financial transactions — to decrypt retroactively once capability exists. This is called "harvest now, decrypt later" (HNDL). Citizens have not consented to this, and may not know it is happening.

This simulator asks: is storing data you cannot yet read a rights violation? Does the answer change if your adversaries are doing the same? Who bears the cost — and who gets protected?

Ethical frameworks applied: Justice as fairness (Rawls) — would you accept these policies if you didn't know whether you were the official or the citizen? Procedural ethics — is secret collection illegitimate regardless of outcome?

Note: All figures are illustrative and designed to communicate the scale of trade-offs, not precise predictions.
